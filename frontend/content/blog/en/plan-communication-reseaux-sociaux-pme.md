## In Brief

A social media communication plan is a structured document that answers six questions: why, for whom, where, what, when, and how to measure. It is not a [content calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar): the calendar breaks the plan down week by week, but does not replace it. And it is not a content strategy either, which covers editorial pillars and brand positioning.

According to the France Num 2025 Barometer, 66% of French businesses are present on at least one social network, but only 46% post at least once a week. The gap usually comes from the absence of a plan: you publish when you think of it, with no direction or measurement.

This guide gives you the 6 building blocks of a workable plan, and a method for building it in three hours.

---

## What a Social Media Communication Plan Must Contain

A good communication plan for a small business fits on one A4 page or a shared spreadsheet. It covers six blocks, in this order:

1. **Goals**: what you want to achieve, with a number and a date.
2. **Audiences**: who you want to reach, described with three questions.
3. **Channels and messages**: where you publish and what you say on each network.
4. **Schedule and organization**: who does what, how many hours per week.
5. **Metrics**: the three numbers you check every month.

Each block is covered below. You can fill them in order, or start with audiences if you are starting from scratch.

---

## Block 1: Set Measurable Goals

A vague goal ("be more visible") drives nothing. A SMART goal does.

**Concrete examples by goal type:**

- **Awareness**: reach 500 LinkedIn followers within 3 months, starting from 120.
- **Traffic**: generate 200 clicks per month to the quote page via Instagram within 6 months.
- **Leads**: receive 10 qualified inbound messages per month on Facebook within 4 months.
- **Recruitment**: publish 2 job listings per month on LinkedIn and receive 5 applications per position.
- **Retention**: maintain a 3% engagement rate on customer posts each quarter.

**Practical rule**: set 1 or 2 goals per network, not five. Too many goals means no real tracking. Choose the one that matches your current business priority.

The most common social media goals for small businesses are acquiring new customers (53%), awareness (47%), and retention (45%), according to a Visiplus Academy study. Start there to choose yours.

---

## Block 2: Define Your Audiences

No need for a 12-page persona. Three questions are enough.

**The 3 simplified persona questions:**

1. **Who are they?** Age, sector, role, situation (head of a 10-person SME, self-employed plumber, solo-practice accountant).
2. **What is their problem?** The one you directly solve (lack of time, difficulty finding customers, not professional enough image).
3. **Where do you find them online?** Main network, time of use, type of content they consume.

**Examples by sector:**

| Sector | Audience | Main Network |
|---|---|---|
| Tradespeople (plumber, electrician) | Homeowners 35-55, local | Facebook, Google |
| Independent professionals (coach, physio) | Working adults 30-50, mid-to-high income | Instagram, LinkedIn |
| B2B service providers (consulting, IT) | SME executives and HR managers | LinkedIn |

Once the audience is defined, the rest of the plan becomes much simpler: the channel almost picks itself, and the message naturally calibrates.

## Block 3: Choose Your Channels and Key Messages

Choose a maximum of 2 channels if you are managing alone. It is better to be consistent on two networks than absent on five.

**Reference table:**

| Channel | Main Audience | Message Types That Perform | Minimum Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | B2B decision-makers, recruitment | Experience, expertise, behind the scenes | 2 posts/week |
| Instagram | General public, visual B2C | Product photos, before/after, testimonials | 3 posts/week |
| Facebook | Local, 35-60 age group | Local news, promotions, customer reviews | 2 posts/week |
| TikTok | 18-35, B2C | Short videos, tutorials, trade humor | 3-5 videos/week |

**On message consistency**: the core message must stay the same across channels, even if the format changes. Your main promise, your tone, your positioning: constant. What changes is the format (short video on TikTok, long text on LinkedIn, square visual on Instagram).

To go further on this point, see the resources on [social media for business owners](/en/blog/social-media-for-business-owners): they detail how to adapt the format without losing brand consistency.

## Block 4: Define Your Schedule and Organization

This is the block most small businesses skip. It is also the one that explains why plans stay in a drawer.

**The three questions to settle:**

- **Who produces the content?** You, a team member, an occasional freelancer?
- **Who approves before publishing?** If it is you, schedule a fixed time slot each week.
- **Who publishes and replies to comments?** That is not the same task as producing content.

**How much time to budget per week?**

- 2 posts/week on 1 network: roughly 1h30 to 2h (writing, visuals, scheduling).
- 3 posts/week on 2 networks: between 3h and 4h, without automation.
- With a [social media management tool](/en/blog/social-media-management-tool-small-business) and ready-made visual templates: down to 1h30.

**Avoiding everything depending on one person:**

- Document the process (who does what, where files are stored, what tone to use).
- Build a stock of 4 to 6 posts in advance each month.
- Use a shared [content calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar), even a simple one (Google Sheets works fine).

If the workload becomes too heavy, the question of [delegating your social media](/en/blog/delegate-social-media-management) comes up. Before deciding, also read the criteria for doing it yourself versus delegating: it all depends on your content volume and budget.

The ideal [publishing frequency](/en/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media) does not exist: what matters is consistency over time, not the volume in any single month.

## Block 5: Measure Without Drowning in Data

Social media dashboards display dozens of metrics. Most of them are useless for a small business.

**The 3 metrics that matter, by goal:**

| Goal | Main Metric | What It Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach | Number of people exposed to your posts |
| Traffic | Clicks to website | Visits generated from social networks |
| Leads | Inbound messages | Qualified contacts received via social networks |
| Retention | Engagement rate | Likes, comments, shares / followers |
| Recruitment | Applications received | Number of files linked to job posts |

**What to check every month:**

- The main metric tied to your goal (just one).
- The number of posts actually published (to measure your consistency).
- The post that performed best (to understand what resonates).

**What to ignore:**

- Follower count in the short term (it fluctuates and does not reflect real performance).
- Impressions without clicks (visibility without action is noise).
- Comparisons with accounts ten times larger than yours.

A 20-minute monthly review is enough. Note the three numbers in a simple table, adjust one parameter, and move on.

## Setting Up Your Plan in a Morning

Three hours, three steps, a usable document by the end of the day.

**Step 1: 60 minutes for the foundations**

Open a document or spreadsheet. Fill in blocks 1 and 2: one SMART goal per network, and your persona in three questions. Do not aim for perfection, aim for clarity.

**Step 2: 60 minutes for the operational plan**

Fill in blocks 3 and 4: choose your 2 channels, note the type of message for each, decide who does what and how much time per week. Add a "content stock" line with 4 post ideas to get started.

**Step 3: 60 minutes for metrics and the first month**

Fill in block 5: one metric per goal. Then plan the first 8 posts of the month in your calendar. You now have an operational social media communication plan, not a theoretical document.

Review it every quarter: goals shift, channels evolve, audiences get clearer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much time does a social media communication plan take to maintain?**

Once built, the plan itself requires no daily upkeep. Allow 20 minutes per month for the metrics review, and a 30-minute check every quarter to adjust goals or channels. Operational time (producing and publishing) is separate from the plan.

**Do I need a different plan for each social network?**

No. One document covers all your networks. What changes from one channel to another is the specific goal, the type of message, and the frequency. Everything else (audience, key messages, organization) stays common.

**How do I know if my plan is working?**

Check your main metric at 90 days. If you set a goal of 10 inbound messages per month and you are getting 3, the problem is either the channel chosen, the message, or the frequency. Change one parameter at a time to identify the blocker.
