## In brief

Creating your Google Business Profile is the starting point. Optimizing it is what decides whether you appear in the local pack or stay invisible. This article does not revisit the creation process (covered in the complete Google Business Profile guide for SMBs). It focuses on the 8 concrete optimization levers, in the order of their impact.

---

## Why optimization changes everything

The Google local pack (the 3 listings displayed at the top of results) captures **42% of clicks** on local searches. Ranking first organically is no longer enough: if you are not in that block of 3, you lose the majority of qualified traffic.

The stakes are straightforward:

- An incomplete listing = Google does not know when to show you.
- A listing with no recent reviews = Google sees you as less trustworthy than an active competitor.
- A listing with no photos = fewer clicks, fewer direction requests.

Listings with photos generate on average **35% more clicks** and **42% more direction requests** than those without visuals. These are not minor details: they are customers choosing a better-presented competitor.

Optimizing your Google listing is the most accessible and fastest **local SEO** lever a small business can act on.

## The 8 optimization points in order of priority

### 1. The primary category

This is the strongest signal you send to Google. Choose the category that exactly matches your main activity, not the most generic one.

- Wrong: "Company" or "Business services"
- Right: "Management consulting firm", "Plumber", "Artisan bakery"

Then add secondary categories to cover your other activities. Google allows up to 10.

### 2. The business name without keyword stuffing

Your name in GBP must match your real trading name. Adding keywords ("Plumber Paris Dupont and Sons") violates Google's rules and exposes your listing to suspension.

Simple rule: if it is not written on your sign or your invoices, do not put it in the name.

### 3. The description in 160 useful characters

The description is not a filler field. It is your hook for the prospect who is on the fence.

Structure it as follows:

1. What you do (main activity)
2. For whom (target audience or geographic area)
3. What sets you apart (turnaround time, guarantee, expertise)

Example: "Accounting firm specializing in micro-businesses and freelancers in Lyon. Annual accounts, payroll, tax advice. Response within 24h, no jargon."

### 4. Photos: quantity and frequency

Aim for **at least 10 to 20 photos** for a solid listing. The long-term goal: 30 photos or more.

Priority categories:

- Exterior of the premises (so customers recognize you on site)
- Interior and work environment
- Team (humanizes the brand)
- Products or concrete work samples
- A polished logo and cover photo

Frequency: add **1 to 2 photos per week**, or at least 3 to 5 per month. Consistency signals an active listing to Google.

### 5. Accurate opening hours

Inaccurate hours drive customers away and hurt your rating. Keep the following up to date:

- Regular hours, hour by hour
- Special hours (public holidays, vacation, events)
- The "Open now" or "Closed" label that displays in real time

A customer who makes a wasted trip will not come back, and sometimes leaves a negative review.

### 6. Attributes and services

Attributes are filters that users apply to refine their search: wheelchair accessibility, contactless payment, parking, online service, etc.

Check all that apply. Add your services with their descriptions and, where possible, their prices. A detailed listing answers questions before the prospect even calls you.

### 7. Customer reviews: getting them and responding

Reviews are the most visible **reputation** signal. They influence both your ranking and the purchasing decision.

**To get reviews:**

- Send the direct link to your listing by SMS or email after each job.
- Ask in person at the moment the customer expresses satisfaction.
- Include the link in your email signature.

**To respond:**

- Reply to every review, positive and negative, within 48 hours.
- Thank reviewers without hollow phrases: mention a specific detail from the job.
- Faced with a negative review: stay factual, offer a solution, do not get defensive.

The frequency and recency of reviews matter as much as the overall rating.

### 8. Google posts: frequency and formats

GBP posts appear directly on your listing. They signal regular activity to Google and give users a reason to click.

**Recommended frequency:** 1 to 2 posts per week. Below one post per week, the listing is perceived as dormant.

**Useful formats:**

- Update: news, tips, behind the scenes
- Offer: a time-limited promotion with a start and end date
- Event: workshop, open day, webinar
- Product or service: spotlight on a specific offering

To structure your publishing schedule, the guides on the [social media editorial calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar) and [posting frequency](/en/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media) provide benchmarks that apply directly to GBP posts.

## The 3 signals Google measures to rank listings

Google states it officially: local ranking rests on three factors. Not ten, not twenty. Three.

**1. Relevance**
Does your listing match what the user is searching for? This is where your category, description, services, and the words that appear in your reviews come into play.

**2. Distance**
Is your business close to where the user is at the time of the search? You cannot move your address, but you can specify your service area.

**3. Prominence**
Google measures your online reputation: number and quality of reviews, mentions on other sites, presence in directories. This is the slowest signal to build, but also the most durable.

Acting on relevance and prominence is exactly what the 8 points above cover. Distance is fixed.

## Monthly checklist

Once the listing is optimized, maintenance takes less than one hour per month:

1. **Check your information**: hours, phone number, website, address.
2. **Add 4 to 8 recent photos** (completed work, team, news).
3. **Publish 4 to 8 posts** (1 to 2 per week).
4. **Reply to all new reviews** received during the month.
5. **Review GBP statistics**: views, clicks, calls, direction requests.

This rhythm is enough to keep a listing active and competitive against competitors who never touch theirs.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to see results after optimizing a Google My Business listing?**

The first effects are generally visible within 4 to 8 weeks. Fast signals (photos, posts, review responses) improve engagement from the first few weeks. Rankings in the local pack shift more slowly, over 2 to 3 months.

**Can you optimize a Google Business Profile without a website?**

Yes. A GBP listing works independently of a website. That said, having a website strengthens the prominence signal and helps Google better understand your activity. It is a complementary lever, not mandatory to get started.

**Do Google posts directly improve local rankings?**

Not directly. Posts have no proven effect on local pack rankings. However, they increase engagement on the listing (clicks, calls), which can indirectly reinforce your relevance in Google's eyes. Their main value remains conversion: turning a listing visitor into a customer.
