## In brief

- Facebook is still relevant for tradespeople, local businesses and B2C activities targeting the 35-55 age group. For B2B or audiences under 30, it is the wrong network.
- Creating a professional Facebook page takes less than an hour. Configuring it properly takes two.
- Organic reach is low (1-3% on average) but real if you choose the right formats.
- 3 posts per week, consistent and varied, are enough for a small business.
- Automation is the only way to maintain that pace without sacrificing your schedule.

## Facebook in 2026: who is it still worth it for?

Facebook has **31.5 million monthly active users in France** in 2026. It is not a dying network - it is a network that has aged, and that is precisely its strength for certain activities.

### Age breakdown in France

| Age group | Share of Facebook audience |
|---|---|
| 25-34 | 26.5% |
| 35-44 | 19.1% |
| 45-54 | 15.4% |
| 55-64 | 12.1% |
| 65 and over | 12.5% |

Users aged 35-64 account for nearly **47% of the audience**. That is the typical customer base for a tradesperson, a local shop, a local service provider or a general B2C business.

### Facebook is worth it if you are:

- **A tradesperson or local business** (plumber, baker, hair salon, florist...)
- **A B2C service provider** targeting the 35-55 age group (coach, therapist, public-facing trainer...)
- **A restaurant or hotel** with a local customer base
- **An association or cultural organisation** with a local community

### Facebook is not worth it if you are:

- **Purely B2B**: LinkedIn is more appropriate; Facebook for business is not the right environment.
- **Primarily targeting under-30s**: Instagram, TikTok and YouTube capture that audience.
- **In the tech or startup space**: your audience is not there.

Asking this question before creating a page saves you from wasting time on the wrong channel.

## Creating a professional Facebook page: the steps in order

You need a personal Facebook account to create a page. This is mandatory - there is no standalone "business" account.

### The 8 steps

1. Log in to your personal Facebook account.
2. In the left menu, click **Pages**, then **Create a Page**.
3. Select **Public page**, click **Next**, then **Get started**.
4. Enter the **page name** (your business name, not your first name) and the **category**.
5. Add a short **bio** (2-3 lines: what you do and who you do it for).
6. Click **Create Page**.
7. Fill in your contact details: address, phone number, website, opening hours.
8. Add your **profile photo** (logo) and **cover photo**.

Allow 30 to 45 minutes for this step. Do not move on without filling in every field - an incomplete page inspires little confidence.

## Key settings to configure

Once the page is created, several settings make the difference between an amateur page and one that converts.

### The category

Choose the most specific category possible. "Plumber" is better than "Local business". Facebook uses it to suggest your page to the right people.

### The long description

Accessible via **Settings > Page info**. Maximum 255 characters. Answer three questions: who you are, what you do, who you do it for. Include your town or geographic area if you operate locally.

### The CTA button

This is the button visible below your cover photo. Useful options depending on your activity:

- **"Call now"** - tradespeople, home services
- **"Send message"** - any business or service provider
- **"Learn more"** - redirect to your website
- **"Book now"** - restaurants, salons, therapists

### The custom URL

Go to **Settings > Edit profile > Username**. Choose `facebook.com/yourname` or `facebook.com/yourbusinessname`. Cleaner to share and easier to remember.

### Tabs

Only enable the ones that are relevant to you: Reviews, Services, Photos, Shop if you sell online. Disable the rest - a page cluttered with empty tabs damages credibility.

## What still generates organic reach in 2026

Organic reach for Facebook pages is low. On average, **1 to 3% of your followers** see a post. That is small, but it is predictable - and some formats perform significantly better than others.

### Formats ranked by organic reach

| Format | Estimated organic reach |
|---|---|
| Reels (short vertical video) | 8-12% |
| Long native video | 4-7% |
| Photo carousel | 3-5% |
| Single photo | 2.5-4% |
| Text only | 2-3% |
| External link | 1-2% |

**Reels are the most effective format** for organic reach in 2026. A 30 to 60-second video, filmed vertically, with subtitles, consistently outperforms every other format.

### What boosts engagement

- Asking a direct question in the post
- Showing behind the scenes of your work (ongoing project, production process, before/after)
- Sharing a customer review with a real photo
- Publishing useful, local content (seasonal tip, practical information for your area)

### The ideal posting frequency for a small business

**3 posts per week** is the right balance: enough to stay visible, few enough to maintain quality. Below one post per week, the algorithm forgets you. Above 5, you risk saturating your audience.

## Managing your page without spending all your time on it

That is the real challenge. Not the setup - keeping it going over time.

### Best practices

**Batch your content.** Set aside 1.5 hours per month to prepare and schedule 12 to 15 posts. Meta Business Suite includes a free scheduling tool.

**Recycle smartly.** A customer review becomes a text post. A project photo becomes a Reel with an [AI voiceover](/en/blog/ai-voiceover-videos-small-business). A seasonal tip from last year gets republished with an update.

**Reply to messages within 24 hours.** Facebook displays your response rate publicly. A rate below 80% damages your credibility.

**Enable automatic replies.** In Meta Business Suite, set up an automatic welcome message for new messages. It does not replace a real reply, but it avoids silence.

A structured [social media editorial calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar) means you never face a blank page on the day you need to post.

If you manage other channels too, the question of [social media management: do it yourself or delegate?](/en/blog/social-media-management-in-house-or-outsource) is worth addressing clearly before you invest your time.

## Mistakes that kill your reach

### Posting external links repeatedly

Facebook penalises posts that send users off the platform. If you share a blog article, include the key content directly in the post and put the link in a comment.

### Ignoring comments

A post with no replies to comments sends a negative signal to the algorithm. Reply to every comment within the first 2 hours - that is the window in which the algorithm decides on reach.

### Posting inconsistently

Three posts in one week, then silence for a month: that is worse than a slow but steady pace. The algorithm rewards consistency.

### Using generic stock photos

Stock images perform poorly. A photo taken with your phone in your workshop or shop generates more engagement - it is authentic and local.

### Neglecting Reviews

Facebook reviews are publicly visible and influence purchasing decisions. Systematically invite satisfied customers to leave a review. Reply to all of them, positive and negative alike.

## FAQ

### Can you create a professional Facebook page without a personal account?

No. Facebook requires a personal account to create and manage a page. Your personal account is not visible on the page - only the page name appears publicly. You can then add other administrators so you are not the only one managing the page.

### What is the difference between a Facebook page and a Facebook profile for a business?

A profile is a personal space. A page is designed for businesses, brands and organisations. A page gives you access to analytics, advertising, post scheduling and Meta's professional tools. Using a personal profile for a commercial activity is against Facebook's terms of use.

### How much time does it take to manage a small business Facebook page each month?

With good organisation, **2 to 3 hours per month** is enough for a local small business: 1.5 hours for content creation and scheduling, 30 minutes for replying to messages and comments. That is the realistic pace for an active page posting 3 times a week.

### Do you need to run Facebook ads to get results?

Not necessarily at the start. Organic reach is low but real if you use the right formats (Reels first). Advertising becomes relevant once you already have a well-configured page, reviews and a clear objective (generating leads, promoting an offer). Running ads on an empty or poorly filled page is wasted money.
