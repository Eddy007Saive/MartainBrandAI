## The essentials

- **Doing it yourself costs time**: between 6 and 10 hours a week for a small business that really commits to it.
- **Delegating costs money**: from 500 € a month for a freelancer to more than 4,500 € fully loaded for an employee.
- **The right choice depends on your situation**: available budget, free time, and the level of consistency you can actually hold.

## Managing your social media yourself: what it really costs

You often hear that social media is free. On paper, that's true. In practice, it's another story.

According to the Bpifrance Le Lab study of 1,650 leaders of small and mid-sized companies, **the number one barrier to an active social media presence is a lack of time**, cited by 84% of the companies surveyed. That is no coincidence.

### Time, the invisible cost

To manage your social media properly, publishing regularly, replying to comments, analysing results, creating visuals, a small business commonly spends **between 6 and 10 hours a week**.

Do the maths. At 10 hours a week, that's **40 hours a month**. If your time is worth 50 € an hour, which is a conservative estimate for a business owner, that comes to **2,000 € of opportunity cost a month**. That's hours not spent on your core business, your clients, your commercial development.

That cost never shows up on a balance sheet. But it is very real.

### The consistency problem

Doing it alone also means fighting against irregularity. When the workload piles up, social media is the first thing to fall by the wayside.

The result: an Instagram page active in January, silent in March, revived in June. That kind of erratic presence hurts more than it helps. The algorithm penalises inconsistency, and visitors who land on an account that has been inactive for three months simply move on.

**Consistency is the hardest skill to hold on your own**, not creativity, not technique. It's the simple act of publishing, week after week, even when you're swamped.

## The three ways to delegate

Delegating your social media management can take three very different forms. Each has its concrete strengths and limits.

### The salaried community manager

This is the most complete solution, and the most expensive. A junior salaried community manager costs between **2,800 and 3,500 € gross a month**, or roughly **4,500 to 5,200 € fully loaded** once employer contributions are added.

For a micro-business or a solo entrepreneur, it's rarely justifiable. This option is for companies that already have a structured digital strategy, several platforms to manage, and a content volume large enough to keep someone busy full-time.

**Its advantage:** total availability, deep knowledge of the company, immediate responsiveness.
**Its limit:** a high fixed cost, even in quiet periods.

### The freelancer

The freelancer is often the best fit for micro and small businesses. Monthly packages generally range from **500 to 1,500 € for basic management**, two or three platforms, three to four posts a week, up to **2,500 to 3,000 €** for full coverage including content creation, advertising and reporting.

The average daily rate recorded by the Malt barometer sits around **400 €**, with wide gaps depending on experience and specialisation.

**Its advantage:** flexibility, cost adjustable to volume, no payroll charges.
**Its limit:** less availability than an employee, and a quality that depends entirely on the brief you give at the outset.

### A tool, with or without support

This is the third path, often underestimated. A scheduling tool lets you automate part of the work, scheduling posts, analysing performance, while the owner keeps control.

This option suits those who want to **keep control of their voice** without spending hours on it. The cost is well below that of a freelancer or an employee.

**Its advantage:** controlled cost, autonomy preserved, consistency made easier.
**Its limit:** a scheduling tool doesn't create the content. It saves you time on publishing, not on writing.

## How to choose based on your situation

There's no one-size-fits-all answer. Here are three concrete profiles to place yourself.

### You have time but no budget

You're starting out, your cash flow is tight, but you can block five to six hours a week. In that case, **managing it yourself with a scheduling tool** is the right option.

Be honest with yourself: if those five hours don't really exist in your calendar, or if they vanish the moment the order book fills up, it isn't a sustainable solution. A modest, regular presence beats an ambitious, abandoned one.

### You have a budget but no time

You're financially comfortable, but every hour counts. Outsourcing to a freelancer or a micro-agency is probably the best value for money.

Plan for **at least 800 to 1,000 € a month** for serious management on two platforms. Below that, you risk getting generic content that doesn't reflect your company. And invest time up front to brief your provider properly: that's where the quality of what gets produced is decided.

### You want a balance between cost and results

This is the most common case. You don't want to delegate everything, because you care about your voice, but you can't spend ten hours a week on it either.

The combination that works: **a tool for consistency, a freelancer a few hours a month for strategy and creation**. You keep control of the exchanges with your community, you delegate the production. Budget between 300 and 700 € a month depending on volume.

## What to keep in mind before deciding

Social media management isn't a question of talent or mastery of tools. It's a question of **consistency and resources**, time or money, the two offset each other.

Four firm points before you decide:

- **Don't underestimate the cost of "I'll do it myself".** Eight hours a week is a full working day. Value it.
- **Don't overestimate what a small budget can buy.** A 200 € a month package won't give you a strategy, just presence.
- **Continuity beats perfection.** An average post published every week beats a perfect post published once a quarter.
- **Test before you commit.** A freelancer on a three-month assignment will cost you less than a bad hire.

There's a fourth path, between the bare tool and full delegation: have your brand framed by humans, tone, editorial line, art direction, then let a platform produce the proposals in that voice, which you review and approve one by one. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much time should you spend on your social media each week?**
For a serious presence on two or three platforms, count on between 6 and 10 hours a week: content creation, scheduling, moderation and analysis. Below three hours, it's hard to hold enough consistency to see visible results.

**Is a freelancer or a salaried community manager better for a small business?**
In the vast majority of cases, the freelancer is the better fit: flexibility, adjustable cost, no fixed charges. An employee makes sense when the volume exceeds three to four days of work a week and you need someone fully immersed in your company.

**Can you delegate your social media without losing your authenticity?**
Yes, provided you brief well. Share your values, your tone, examples of content you like, and the topics never to touch. Authenticity is built in the brief, not in writing everything yourself.
