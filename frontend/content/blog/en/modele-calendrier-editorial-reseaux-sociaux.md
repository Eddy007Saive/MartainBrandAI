## The columns of a good social media editorial calendar

A good social media content calendar fits in a single table. No need for ten tabs. Here are the 9 columns that are enough.

| Column | What goes in it | Why it is useful |
|---|---|---|
| **Date** | Planned publication date | Lets you spot gaps and avoid duplicates on the same day |
| **Network** | LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, GBP | Filter by channel in one click |
| **Format** | Carousel, Reel, text post, short video, photo, update | Anticipates the production time needed |
| **Content pillar** | E.g.: expertise, behind the scenes, testimonial, offer | Ensures consistency with your editorial line |
| **Topic / Angle** | The sentence that summarizes the post | Avoids starting from scratch when it is time to write |
| **Text or script** | The final text or key points | Centralizes everything in one place, no last-minute searching |
| **Visual** | Link to the file or description of the visual to create | Avoids oversights and makes delegation easier |
| **Status** | To do / In progress / To approve / Published | Essential as soon as another person touches the calendar |
| **Results** | Reach, engagement, clicks (to fill in after publication) | Allows you to adjust the content strategy the following month |

**Practical tip:** in Google Sheets, use a dropdown list for the Network, Format, Pillar, and Status columns. In Notion, use a database with "Select" type properties. You save time with every entry.

## How to fill your calendar in 30 minutes a month

The method has 4 steps. Do them in order, without getting sidetracked.

**Step 1: Choose your content pillars (5 minutes)**

Before listing topics, ask yourself one question: what do you want to be known for? Define 3 to 5 stable pillars, for example: industry expertise, behind the scenes, client testimonials, offers and news, point of view. These pillars come from your editorial line and do not change from month to month.

**Step 2: List 8 to 10 topics in rough form (10 minutes)**

Open a blank page. Write down everything that happened in your business the previous month: a repeated client question, a completed project, a problem solved, an industry development, a notable figure. Do not filter yet. Aim for 10 raw ideas.

**Step 3: Distribute by channel and by week (10 minutes)**

Take your 10 topics and place them in the table. Simple rule: a strong topic goes first on LinkedIn or Instagram. Behind-the-scenes content works well on TikTok and Instagram. Offers and news go on Facebook and GBP. Spread them across 4 weeks while sticking to your target frequency.

**Step 4: Duplicate or adapt a topic across multiple channels (5 minutes)**

Identify 2 or 3 topics worth repurposing. A LinkedIn post becomes an Instagram carousel. A TikTok video becomes a GBP update with a screenshot. This recycling is the key to maintaining a social media publication schedule without doubling your production time.

## The mistakes that make a calendar unusable

A poorly designed publication dashboard is worse than having no calendar at all: it creates the illusion of being organized without actually being so.

- **Too many columns.** If your table has more than 12 columns, nobody fills it in. Start with the 9 columns listed above, and add one only if you have a concrete need for it.
- **Empty fields with no owner.** If the "Visual" column stays empty because nobody knows who is supposed to create it, the post will not go out. Every row must have an identified person responsible.
- **No validation status.** Without a Status column, you do not know what is ready and what is not. The result: you publish in a rush or you miss dates.
- **Too infrequent reviews.** A social media calendar filled in once a quarter is already outdated within two weeks. Once a month is the right rhythm.
- **Calendar disconnected from business news.** If an important event comes up (launch, trade show, hiring), your calendar must be able to absorb it. Always keep 2 to 3 open slots per month for the unexpected.

## Keeping your calendar going over time: the 3 habits that make the difference

A good communication plan does not hold together through motivation. It holds together through short, regular habits.

**1. The 30-minute monthly review**

At the start of each month, open your calendar. Look at the previous month's results (Results column), identify the formats that performed best, and adjust the pillars if needed. This review takes 30 minutes, no more. It replaces all the "content strategy" meetings that lead nowhere.

**2. A creation batch once a week**

Set aside a fixed slot, 1 to 2 hours, to write the texts and prepare the visuals for the following week. Working in batches is twice as fast as creating one post at a time. Your brain stays in the same mode of thinking, and you do not waste time getting back up to speed.

**3. Delegate publication without delegating approval**

This is the most important rule. You can hand off formatting, scheduling, and tracking to someone else. But content approval stays on your side. A post published under your name puts your reputation on the line. [Automating](/en/blog/social-media-automation) publications can handle the technical side, as long as you have approved every piece of content before it goes out.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between an editorial calendar and a publication schedule?**

The editorial calendar is the management document: it contains the topics, angles, formats, and statuses. The social media publication schedule is the operational view: the exact dates and times of each post. In practice, both can live in the same table.

**How long does it take to fill in an editorial calendar each month?**

With the 4-step method described in this article, 30 minutes is enough to plan a full month across 5 channels. The condition: having your content pillars already defined and a list of raw topics ready before you sit down at the table.

**Is it better to use Google Sheets or Notion for a social media calendar?**

Both work. Google Sheets is faster to share and filter. Notion offers more flexibility with views (board, calendar, list). Choose the tool you already use daily: the best calendar is the one you actually open.
