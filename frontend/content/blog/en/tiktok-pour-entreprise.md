## In Brief

TikTok for business is the platform with the strongest organic reach in 2026, but it is not the right choice for everyone. If you are a tradesperson, retailer, or service provider with a visual offer, it is probably the most effective lever for reaching new customers without an advertising budget. If you are purely B2B or in highly specialized consulting, your time is better spent elsewhere. This guide helps you decide, then get started if the answer is yes.

---

## TikTok in 2026: The Figures That Matter for a Small Business

**27 million monthly active users in France.** That is the reference figure for 2026, representing around 40% of French internet users. The platform is no longer reserved for teenagers.

The real demographics:

- 18-24 years old: 30 to 35% of users
- 25-34 years old: 25 to 28%
- 35-54 years old: 28 to 32%
- 55 and older: 8 to 12%

**60% of French users are over 25.** The "TikTok = teens" cliche dates back to 2021.

Three figures that directly concern a small business:

- **49%** of users say they discover a new brand on TikTok every month.
- **38%** made a purchase directly triggered by content they saw on the platform in the past 12 months.
- **41%** search for a brand on TikTok before buying, the way they used to do on Google.

On organic reach, the comparison is clear:

| Platform | Organic reach in 2026 | What it means for a small business |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | The strongest, distributed to non-followers | Fast visibility without an ad budget |
| Instagram | Declining, dependent on Reels | Good for relationships, less for discovery |
| Facebook | Very low on pages (1 to 5%) | Useful for community and paid, not for organic reach |

TikTok distributes your videos to people who do not know you yet. That is its fundamental difference.

## Who TikTok Is Really Worth It For (and Who Should Prioritize Something Else)

A clear position: TikTok is not universal. Here is how to decide.

**Sectors where TikTok performs well:**

- Tradespeople and manual crafts (carpenter, plumber, baker, hairdresser)
- Local retail and brick-and-mortar shops
- Restaurants and food businesses
- Visual professions (interior designer, photographer, decorator)
- Wellness, fitness, coaching
- B2C e-commerce with a demonstrable product

**Sectors where TikTok is not the priority:**

- Pure B2B with long sales cycles
- Legal, financial, or HR consulting for businesses
- Highly regulated sectors (healthcare, finance) with strict communication constraints
- Activities with no visual or narrative dimension

**Three questions to help you decide:**

1. Can your offer be shown in 20 seconds? If yes, TikTok is for you.
2. Are your customers between 18 and 54 years old? If yes, they are on TikTok.
3. Do you have time to film 2 to 3 short videos per week, or someone to do it? If not, start with a professional Facebook page or explore [Instagram for business](/en/blog/instagram-for-business) first.

## How the TikTok Algorithm Works (What Actually Matters)

The TikTok algorithm does not work like Facebook or Instagram. **Your follower count does not determine your reach.** A video from an account with 50 followers can reach 50,000 people if the signals are right.

**The mechanism in three steps:**

1. TikTok shows your video to a small group (a few hundred people).
2. It measures engagement signals during the first few hours.
3. If the signals are positive, it expands distribution in successive waves.

**The signals that matter, in order:**

- **Completion rate**: do people watch all the way through? Above 75%, distribution takes off. Below 50%, it stops.
- **Shares**: especially direct message sends. This is the strongest signal according to recent analyses.
- **Comments**: they indicate that the video generates a reaction.
- **Likes and saves**: useful, but less decisive than the three above.

**What does not matter (or matters little):**

- The number of followers on your account.
- Your past posting frequency.
- The time of posting (the impact is marginal).

**Concrete example:** a plumber who films a repair in 25 seconds with an identifiable problem in the first second, a clear solution, and a hook like "are you making this mistake at home?" gets a high completion rate, shares between neighbors, and comments. The TikTok algorithm distributes.

## Formats That Work for a Business

The short business video that performs on TikTok follows a simple structure: a strong hook in 2 seconds, value delivered in 15 to 25 seconds, a clear conclusion.

**By sector:**

- **Tradesperson**: "before / after" a renovation, a common mistake to avoid, behind the scenes on a job site. Format: 20 to 30 seconds, filmed on a phone, no elaborate editing.
- **Local retailer**: new product arrivals, a product demonstration, "here is what we are preparing for the week". Format: 15 to 25 seconds, natural tone.
- **Service provider**: a misconception about your profession, a practical tip in 3 points, an answer to a question your clients often ask. Format: 20 to 35 seconds, facing the camera or on-screen text.
- **Self-employed professional**: demystifying a complex topic, an anonymized real-world case, "what most people do not know about...". Format: 25 to 40 seconds, educational tone.

**What kills a TikTok video:**

- A 5-second intro featuring your logo.
- A tone that is too institutional or too formal.
- A video that opens with "Hello, today I am going to talk to you about...".

## Creating a TikTok Business Account: The Key Settings

**Business account vs personal account:** the Business account gives access to detailed analytics, clickable contact information in the profile, and TikTok for Business tools. It is the required starting point for any small business.

**How to create a TikTok business account:**

1. Create a personal account using a professional email address.
2. Go to: Profile > Menu > Settings and privacy > Account > Switch to Business Account.
3. Choose the category that best describes your activity (retail, services, food and beverage, etc.).

**Settings to configure immediately:**

- **Bio**: 80 characters maximum, one sentence that says what you do and for whom. No vague tagline.
- **Link**: point to your website or a direct contact page.
- **Category**: choose the most precise option available; it influences initial distribution.
- **Contact info**: enable email and phone in the Business account settings.
- **Privacy**: keep the account public. A private account is useless for a business.

**Profile photo:** your logo or your photo if you are the face of your brand. Avoid visuals that are too busy; they are unreadable at small sizes.

## Posting Consistently Without It Taking Over Your Evenings

A realistic frequency for a small business: **2 to 3 videos per week**. Below that, you do not enter your followers' daily feed. Above that, you burn out and quality drops.

**Three practices that save time:**

- **Batch filming**: film 6 to 8 videos in a 90-minute session, once every two weeks. Same setting, same outfit, different topics.
- **Repurposing from Reels**: a video already published on Instagram Reels can be republished on TikTok with a different title. The content is the same; the distribution reaches a different audience.
- **A simple [editorial calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar)**: 4 recurring themes (practical tip, behind the scenes, before/after, customer question), 2 videos per week, planned in advance. You never start from scratch.

When it comes to [posting frequency](/en/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media), the goal is not to post every day but to keep going over time. A pace of 2 videos per week for 6 months is worth more than 10 videos in January and nothing in March.

[Social media for business owners](/en/blog/social-media-for-business-owners) that works over the long term is built on systems, not on motivation of the moment. [Automating](/en/blog/social-media-automation) scheduling and content generation makes it possible to maintain that pace without giving up your evenings.

## FAQ

**Is TikTok suitable for B2B small businesses?**

Rarely. TikTok is a B2C discovery platform. If your customers are individuals or small businesses reachable through the general public, yes. If you sell to procurement departments or large accounts, LinkedIn will be more effective for your time.

**Do you have to appear on screen?**

No. On-screen text formats, product demonstration videos, and "before/after" videos without a face on camera work very well. Authenticity matters more than physical presence. That said, accounts where a person speaks directly to camera generally have a higher completion rate.

**How long before you see results?**

Allow 6 to 8 weeks of consistent posting before you have usable data. Some videos take off in the first week; others take a month. The TikTok algorithm can redistribute an older video at any time if it suddenly receives engagement. Consistency is the only variable you control.
