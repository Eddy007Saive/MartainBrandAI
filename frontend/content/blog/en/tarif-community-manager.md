## The essentials

- A freelance community manager costs between **250 and 500 € per day** depending on experience, or between **800 and 3,500 € per month** on a package.
- The price depends mostly on the number of networks, the volume of content, and whether or not there is a strategy to build.
- A good freelancer is expensive. A bad one is useless. The real question is the ratio between what you pay and what you get.

## What a community manager really does

A freelance community manager is not someone who "posts on Instagram". It is a professional who runs your presence over a scope defined with you.

Their work usually covers:

- **Content creation**: text, visuals, short videos, carousels, adapted to each platform.
- **Community management and moderation**: replies to comments, private messages, interaction with the community.
- **Monitoring**: what your competitors do, the trends, the weak signals.
- **Reporting**: engagement rate, reach, community growth, and the adjustments that follow.

What they do **not** do by default: your website's search ranking, your Google Ads campaigns, your newsletters, or the overhaul of your visual identity. If you expect all of that, spell it out in the brief, and adjust the budget.

A good freelancer asks questions from the very first exchange. If they accept everything without blinking, that is a signal.

## The going rates

### The day rate

In France, the day rate of a freelance community manager sits between **250 and 500 €** for the vast majority of profiles.

| Experience | Observed day rate |
| --- | --- |
| Junior (0 to 2 years) | 200 – 300 € |
| Experienced (3 to 7 years) | 300 – 400 € |
| Senior (8 years and more) | 400 – 500 € |
| Sector specialist | 500 € and up |

The Malt barometer puts the national average around **310 to 407 € per day** for the most common profiles. Most active freelancers have more than five years of experience, so expect quotes at the top of that range.

The day rate applies mostly to one-off assignments: launching an account, crisis management, an event campaign. For an ongoing presence, most freelancers offer a monthly package.

### Monthly packages

This is the most common model for a long-term engagement.

| Level | Monthly rate | What is included |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | 500 – 900 € | 1 network, 8 to 12 posts per month, light moderation |
| Standard | 900 – 1,500 € | 2 to 3 networks, editorial plan, simple reporting |
| Advanced | 1,500 – 3,500 € | Multi-network, strategy, video creation, detailed reporting |

**Below 800 € per month**, it is hard to get consistent work across two networks. It is simple arithmetic: if the freelancer charges 350 € per day, 800 € covers barely two days of work per month, including creation, publishing and moderation.

### What makes the price vary

Three levers explain most of the differences.

1. **Experience and specialisation.** A profile specialised in B2B or in online retail, with measurable results to show, will charge more than a beginner generalist. That is normal.
2. **The scope.** Running one LinkedIn account with three posts a week has nothing to do with four networks, video, daily moderation and a monthly report.
3. **The location.** A Paris-based freelancer charges on average 15 to 20 % more than a profile in the regions. The gap narrows with remote work, but it still exists.

Other factors weigh in: the reactivity expected, seven days a week or weekdays only?, the working language, and whether or not there is a strategy to build from scratch.

## Freelancer, agency or tool: what sets them apart

### The freelancer

**Their strengths:** the relationship is direct and the cost is lower than an agency for the same scope. You talk to the person doing the work, not to a project manager who passes messages along.

**Their limits:** a single pair of hands. If they are on holiday or ill, continuity is not guaranteed. And if you need video, design and paid advertising all at once, you will need either a very versatile profile, or several providers.

**Who it is for:** small businesses, SMEs and young companies with one or two networks to run, and a budget of 800 to 2,500 € per month.

### The agency

An agency brings a team: a project manager, one or more community managers, sometimes a designer and an advertising specialist. That is its added value, and that is what you pay for.

Rates start around **900 to 1,500 € per month** at the entry level, and climb quickly to **3,000 to 8,000 €** for a full service with video production and sponsored campaigns.

**Its strengths:** guaranteed continuity, varied resources, the ability to scale up quickly.

**Its limits:** the price, clearly higher than a freelancer for a comparable scope. And a less direct relationship: you are also funding the structure, the meetings and the margins.

**Who it is for:** companies with several platforms, regular video, or complex paid campaigns.

### A tool, with or without support

The third option, often dismissed too quickly. A tool lets you schedule, publish and analyse from a single interface. The monthly cost is counted in tens of euros, not thousands.

**Its strengths:** a cost on a completely different scale from the other two options, and the autonomy you keep.

**Its limits:** a scheduling tool does not create the content. If nobody writes, nothing happens. That is the difference between a tool that schedules and a service that produces.

**Who it is for:** organisations where someone already writes, and who lose time on publishing rather than on creation.

## How to tell whether the budget is worth it

### The questions to ask before signing

Judge the quality of the answers as much as their content.

- **Which networks do you manage, and how often ?** A good freelancer answers with precise figures, not with "it depends".
- **Who creates the visuals and the videos ?** You, them, or a subcontractor: and at what cost?
- **What report will I receive, and at what pace ?** A monthly dashboard with the key indicators is the minimum.
- **Do you have references in my sector ?** Someone who already knows your audience's codes starts with a head start.
- **What happens if you are unavailable ?** The answer says a lot about how serious the profile is.

### The warning signs that should stop you

- The quote stays vague on the number of posts, the networks included and the exact deliverables.
- You are promised guaranteed results in followers or in sales. Nobody can guarantee that.
- No portfolio, no verifiable references, or a personal account left empty for six months.
- They accept everything without asking a single question about your audience, your goals or your tone.
- The rate is abnormally low: less than 200 € per month to "manage everything". At that price, either the work is rushed, or someone is learning on your networks.

A good freelancer sometimes turns down an assignment that is not a fit. That is a sign of maturity, not a flaw.

## What to remember

The rate of a freelance community manager runs from 250 to 500 € per day, or from 800 to 3,500 € per month on a package, depending on experience, scope and location. It is an investment, not an expense: a good profile who generates qualified engagement and protects your reputation earns more than they cost.

The agency costs more but brings a team and continuity. The tool costs far less, but assumes that someone writes.

So the real question is not "how do I pay less?" but "what level of results do I want, and what budget am I willing to put into it?".

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the average rate of a freelance community manager?**
Between 300 and 407 € per day for an experienced profile of three to eight years, according to the Malt barometer. On a monthly package, count on 800 to 1,500 € for standard management of two to three networks.

**Is it better to go through a freelancer or an agency?**
It depends on the scope. The freelancer suits a small business or an SME with one or two networks and a budget under 2,500 € per month. The agency becomes relevant when you need a full team, strategy, video, paid advertising, and guaranteed continuity, but its price is clearly higher.

**How do I know whether a freelancer is genuinely competent?**
Ask for a portfolio with quantified results: engagement rate, community growth, campaign examples. Look at their own accounts. And start with a short assignment or an audit of your current presence before committing for the long term.
