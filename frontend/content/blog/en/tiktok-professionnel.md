## In brief

This article is for SMB owners who have decided to get started on TikTok and want to know exactly how to do it. If you are still trying to convince yourself the platform is worth it, start with our guide on TikTok for businesses. Here, we get straight to action: which account type to choose, how to set it up, how the algorithm works, and how to post without it becoming a second job.

## Personal, creator, or business account: which one to choose

TikTok offers three account types. Here is what sets them apart:

| Account type | Advantages | Limitations | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | Access to all sounds (including commercial music), full social features | No analytics, no business tools | Private use only |
| **Creator** | Advanced analytics, access to popular sounds, monetization tools | Not designed to promote a brand | Influencers, independent content creators |
| **Business** | Full analytics, clickable link in bio, advertising tools, business category | Limited access to commercial music (dedicated sound library) | SMBs, brands, owners representing their company |

**For an SMB, the answer is: Business account.** You get access to performance stats, you can add a link in your bio from the start, and you benefit from tools built for TikTok business use. The music restriction is not an issue: the commercial sound library is large enough to find what you need.

## Setting up your professional TikTok account: steps in order

### 1. Create the basic account

- Download the TikTok app (iOS or Android).
- Sign up with a professional email address or a phone number.
- Avoid signing in through a personal Google or Apple account: you will lose management flexibility if you hand the account over to someone else.

### 2. Switch to a Business account

- Go to **Profile**, then the menu in the top right corner.
- Open **Settings and privacy**, then **Manage account**.
- Select **Switch to Business Account**.
- Choose the category that matches your activity (e.g., Professional Services, E-commerce, Education).

### 3. Configure the key settings

- **Bio**: 80 characters maximum, use all of them (see next section).
- **Link**: add your website or contact page, not your generic homepage.
- **Profile picture**: logo or a photo of the owner, square, neutral background, readable at small size.
- **Privacy**: set the account to **public** immediately. A private account is useless for a content strategy.
- **Notifications**: turn off non-essential alerts so you are not interrupted every hour.

## Optimizing your profile to be found

TikTok increasingly works like a search engine. The words you put in your profile influence who finds you.

**Your username**: choose something consistent with your brand, without unnecessary underscores or numbers. Ideally, the same as on [Instagram for business](/en/blog/instagram-for-business) and your other channels. Consistency makes you easier to remember.

**The bio in 80 characters**: be direct about what you do and who you do it for. Example: "Interior designer Lyon | Full renovation | Free quote". Include a professional keyword, not a vague tagline.

**The link**: point to a page that converts, a contact page, a quote form, a landing page. Not the homepage.

**The photo or logo**: if you are the face of the brand, use your photo. Accounts with a real human face generate more trust than a logo alone, especially on TikTok.

## Understanding the TikTok algorithm in 5 points

The TikTok algorithm distributes each video to a small test audience, then expands reach gradually based on the signals it receives. Here is what actually matters in 2026:

1. **Completion rate**: this is the strongest signal. If people watch your video to the end (or replay it), TikTok pushes it to more people. Short, dense videos have a natural advantage here.

2. **Shares**: a share is worth more than a like. When someone sends your video to a friend, it is a strong signal that the content has real value.

3. **Comments**: they indicate that the video triggered a reaction. An open question at the end of a video is usually enough to generate some.

4. **Negative signals**: if users click "Not interested" or drop off within the first 2 seconds, the algorithm reduces distribution. That is why the first 3 seconds are critical.

5. **Hashtags**: they help categorize your content, they do not create virality. Use 3 to 5 specific, thematic hashtags, not 20 generic ones. The TikTok algorithm also reads the words you say, the on-screen text, and the caption: pay as much attention to those as to your hashtags.

## Your first pieces of content to publish

For an SMB just starting out, three formats work well and require no advanced technical skills.

### Format 1: the trade demonstration (15 to 30 seconds)

Show what you do, not what you say you do. A plumber showing a badly fitted joint and the fix in 20 seconds. A consultant explaining a common mistake in 3 steps. A short business video showing the concrete reality converts better than a speech.

Structure for the first 3 seconds: start with the result or the problem, not your introduction. "Here is why your energy bill is too high" rather than "Hi, my name is...".

### Format 2: question and answer (20 to 45 seconds)

Answer a real question your clients ask you often. You already have the content in your head, you just need to film it. This format also works well for TikTok search visibility, since people look for answers directly in the app.

### Format 3: behind the scenes (15 to 30 seconds)

Show your workshop, your office, a delivery, a meeting. No script needed. This format humanizes the brand and generates natural engagement.

**Recommended length to start**: between 15 and 45 seconds. Short enough to maximize the completion rate, long enough to deliver value.

## A realistic posting routine for a business owner

### The minimum effective frequency

**3 videos per week** is the right starting point for an SMB. It is enough for the algorithm to test you regularly, without burning you out. Check out our article on [posting frequency](/en/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media) to adapt this pace to your situation.

Below 2 videos per week, progress is too slow. Above 5, quality often drops and the owner gives up after 3 weeks.

### Batch filming

Do not film one video at a time. Set aside 1.5 hours per week to shoot 3 to 4 videos in one go. Same location, same outfit, same lighting. You save time and maintain visual consistency.

Prepare your topics in advance with a simple [editorial calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar): 10 ideas saved in a file, and you pick from it each week.

### Repurposing from Instagram

If you are already posting Instagram Reels, reuse them on TikTok. Remove the Instagram watermark before publishing (TikTok penalizes videos bearing another platform's logo). The content is the same, and your production effort is cut in half.

## FAQ

**Can you switch back from a Business account to a Personal account?**

Yes, TikTok lets you change account type in the settings at any time. You do not lose your videos or your followers. However, you lose access to analytics and business tools.

**Do you need a separate TikTok Ads account to run advertising?**

Yes. The professional TikTok account (Business) you create in the app is separate from TikTok Ads Manager. To run paid campaigns, you need to create an account at ads.tiktok.com and link it to your profile.

**Can a professional TikTok account with few followers still reach a large audience?**

Yes, and that is actually one of TikTok's advantages over other platforms. The algorithm distributes each video to a test audience regardless of follower count. An SMB with 200 followers can easily get 10,000 views on a video if the completion rate is good.
