You know you need to be present on social media. You also know you no longer have the time to do it seriously. So you start thinking about **delegating your social media**, but to whom? And how far, without losing your voice?

That is exactly the right question. And the answer depends on your profile, your budget, and your tolerance for the risk of misalignment.

---

**TL;DR**

- Managing your social media alone takes 20-30 hours a week: that is incompatible with an executive's schedule.
- You can delegate the mechanics (visuals, scheduling, reporting). You cannot delegate your point of view.
- There are 4 options: freelance community manager, agency, in-house community manager, calibrated AI. Each suits a specific profile.
- Before deciding who, prepare your delegation in 5 steps, otherwise you will get generic content, even with the best provider.

---

## Why delegating your social media becomes unavoidable for an executive

### Social media is a full-time job

In 2026, doing good social media takes **at least 20 to 30 hours a week** (SocialSky). This is not an estimate: it is the actual time needed to produce quality content, stay up to date on algorithms, manage the community, and analyze results.

**Consistency** requires 5 to 7 posts a week, every day of the year, impossible to keep up alone when your priority is running a company.

And the results confirm it: **brands that handle everything in-house get results 5 to 10 times worse** than those relying on outside expertise (SocialSky, 2026).

### What it costs not to delegate

- Brain time taken away from your core business
- Irregularity that penalizes the algorithm
- Sub-optimal content for lack of mastery of current formats
- An image perceived as less professional than it really is

### But delegating to just anyone carries risks

- Loss of voice and misalignment with your real positioning
- Dependence on a single provider
- Cost with no clear ROI if the objectives are not defined
- Standardized content that looks like every one of your competitors'

> **Key takeaway:** the question is not "should I delegate?" For most executives, the answer is yes. The real question is: to whom, how far, and with what safeguards?

## What you can delegate, and what must stay you

### What you can delegate without risk

- Producing visuals and formatting posts
- Scheduling and publishing at the best times
- Competitive and industry monitoring
- Tracking metrics and producing reports
- Handling generic comments (thanking, redirecting)
- Researching content ideas and first drafts

### What must stay you, no matter what

- **Your point of view:** your opinions on your industry, your convictions, your stances
- **Your lived experience:** your client anecdotes, your mistakes, your lessons learned, no one can invent them
- **Final validation:** every post published under your name puts your reputation on the line
- **Strategy:** which objectives, which networks, which positioning, that is your call
- **Sensitive interactions:** replying to a negative comment, handling a crisis, engaging a hot prospect in DMs

> **Tip, the golden rule:** delegate the mechanics, keep the voice. Anything that can be done without knowing you personally can be delegated. Anything that requires your experience, your opinion, or your judgment must stay under your control.

## The 4 options for delegating your social media, an honest comparison

There is no single way to hand off your social media. Here are the 4 available options, with their advantages, their limits, and the profile each one suits.

### Option 1, the freelance community manager

**Profile:** an independent writer/strategist specialized in social media, often expert on 1-2 networks.

**What they do:** editorial strategy, content creation, scheduling, community management, reporting.

**Observed rates:** 500 to 2,000 EUR/month depending on volume and experience (MissComm, ZeTruc).

**Advantages:**
- Flexibility and a direct relationship
- Fast adaptation to your feedback
- Lower cost than an agency

**Risks:**
- Dependence on a single person
- Frequent turnover in the sector
- Variable quality depending on the profile

**Ideal for:** SMBs with a budget of 500-1,500 EUR/month, 1-2 networks to manage, a need for flexibility.

---

### Option 2, the social media agency

**Profile:** a team with complementary profiles, strategist, creative, data analyst, ads manager.

**What it does:** complete strategy, content production, multi-network management, advertising, advanced reporting.

**Observed rates:** 1,500 to 5,000 EUR/month depending on size and services (SocialSky).

**Advantages:**
- Multidisciplinary expertise
- Professional tools included (2,000 to 4,000 EUR/month of tools, scheduling, analytics, content creation, sentiment analysis)
- Guaranteed continuity of service
- Constant algorithm monitoring: **80% of an agency's time** goes to staying up to date on algorithms, formats, and statistics (SocialSky)

**Risks:**
- High cost
- Risk of content standardization
- Multiple points of contact
- Contracts often 6-12 months

**Ideal for:** SMBs with a budget of 2,000 EUR+/month, a multi-network presence, a need for paid advertising and structured reporting.

---

### Option 3, the in-house community manager (part-time or apprenticeship)

**Profile:** an employee dedicated to digital communication, often on an apprenticeship or part-time.

**What they do:** content creation, scheduling, community management, basic reporting.

**Cost:** 800 to 1,500 EUR/month (apprentice) up to 2,500-3,500 EUR/month (full-time junior CM).

**Advantages:**
- Immersion in the company culture
- Responsiveness and on-the-ground knowledge
- Gradual skill-building

**Risks:**
- Need for regular training and supervision
- High turnover among junior profiles
- Content that is too "institutional" without a strong editorial framework

**Ideal for:** SMBs with a high content volume, several networks, and a real capacity to supervise a junior profile.

---

### Option 4, AI calibrated on your voice

**Profile:** an AI tool configured on your tone, your values, your recurring examples, and your positioning.

**What it does:** generate first drafts of posts from your raw ideas, repurpose existing content, suggest editorial angles, schedule publications.

**Cost:** 50 to 200 EUR/month depending on the tool.

**Advantages:**
- Minimal cost
- Available 24/7, no delay
- No dependence on a human third party
- Voice preserved if well configured
- Immediate results from the very first month

**Risks:**
- Requires a good initial briefing (about 2h of setup)
- Validation remains essential
- Does not replace strategic thinking

**Executive time:** 45 min/week (validation + adjustments) instead of 3 to 4h.

**Ideal for:** an executive with a limited budget (under 300 EUR/month), who wants to keep full control of their voice, who publishes mainly on 1-2 networks.

> This option is often overlooked, wrongly. For an executive whose personal branding is central, AI calibrated on their voice offers an unbeatable control/cost/time ratio.

## How to prepare the delegation in 5 steps (before choosing who)

### Step 1, audit your current presence

Start with an honest assessment:

- Which networks do you have? Which ones generate results? Which ones are abandoned?
- Quick SWOT analysis: strengths (content that performs), weaknesses (irregularity, missing formats), opportunities (untapped networks), threats (more active competitors)

Use each network's native insights and Google Search Console for traffic data (ZeTruc).

### Step 2, define your business objectives (not your social media objectives)

Awareness? Leads? Recruitment? Retention? Direct sales?

Each objective implies a different strategy, and a different provider profile. If you do not know what you want to achieve, no one will be able to deliver it.

### Step 3, write your editorial guidelines (the essential document)

This is the step most executives skip, and it is the main reason delegation fails.

A minimal set of editorial guidelines contains:

- Your positioning in 2 sentences
- Your tone (examples of phrasings you use vs. ones you never use)
- Your 3-4 content pillars
- Your forbidden topics (competitors not to mention, sensitive subjects, stances to avoid)
- Examples of your successful posts (concrete references)

Without these guidelines, you will get generic content, even with the best provider (MissComm).

### Step 4, choose your provider with the right criteria

**Key criteria:** experience in your sector, verifiable client references, a clear work process, smooth communication, transparency on metrics (ZeTruc).

**Red flags to avoid:**
- Promises of guaranteed results
- No editorial strategy in the proposal
- Abnormally low prices
- No onboarding phase planned

**Golden rule:** test before you commit. Ask for a one-month trial or a short assignment before any long contract (MissComm). A test phase is essential to make sure the content produced is genuinely aligned with your voice.

### Step 5, set up tracking and checkpoints

- **Validation frequency:** weekly at first, then bi-monthly once trust is established
- **Monthly KPIs:** reach, engagement, leads generated via social, quality of applications (if employer branding)
- **Quarterly recalibration point:** does the published content still sound like you?

## The 5 mistakes that make a social media delegation fail

1. **Delegating without editorial guidelines** - the provider invents your voice, and it shows.
2. **Choosing the cheapest without checking references** - short-term saving, long-term damage to your image.
3. **Delegating 100% without ever proofreading** - a poorly calibrated post published under your name can be costly in credibility.
4. **Not defining clear business objectives** - impossible to measure the ROI of outsourced social media management without a defined target.
5. **Switching providers every 3 months** - editorial continuity is an asset. Starting from scratch is an often underestimated hidden cost.

## How far to delegate? The 3-level rule

### Level 1, partial delegation (recommended to start)

**You keep:** strategy, ideas, final validation.

**You delegate:** formatting, scheduling, visuals, reporting.

**Time freed up:** from 3-4h to 1h/week.

**Ideal for:** an executive who wants to test risk-free, a limited budget, strong personal branding.

---

### Level 2, supervised delegation (the right balance for most SMBs)

**You keep:** final validation, sensitive interactions, strategic stances.

**You delegate:** writing the posts (based on your raw ideas), handling routine comments, scheduling, reporting.

**Time freed up:** from 3-4h to 30-45 min/week.

**Ideal for:** SMBs with a community manager or calibrated AI, a budget of 200-1,500 EUR/month.

---

### Level 3, full delegation (reserve for specific cases)

**You delegate:** everything, including strategy and interactions.

**Major risk:** gradual misalignment between your real voice and your published voice, detected by your audience within 3 to 6 months.

**When it is acceptable:**
- Company page (not personal profile)
- Institutional communication
- A highly regulated sector where the personal voice matters less

**When it is risky:**
- The executive's personal branding
- A sector where the human relationship is central (consulting, coaching, B2B services)
- Any situation where your audience follows *you*, not your brand

## FAQ

**How much does it cost to delegate your social media?**

It all depends on the option chosen. A freelance CM costs between 500 and 2,000 EUR/month. An SMB social media agency, between 1,500 and 5,000 EUR/month. A CM on an apprenticeship, between 800 and 1,500 EUR/month. AI calibrated on your voice, between 50 and 200 EUR/month. The right budget is the one that matches your objectives, not the lowest nor the highest.

**Can you delegate your social media without losing your voice?**

Yes, provided you prepare the delegation properly. Solid editorial guidelines, a test phase, and regular content validation are enough to maintain alignment. The voice gets lost when you delegate without a framework, not when you delegate well.

**Should you delegate all your networks or only some?**

Start with the networks that cost you the most time and generate the fewest results. Some executives manage their LinkedIn very well in-house and hand their social media on other platforms to a provider. Partial delegation is often the best entry point.

**When should you change providers?**

When the published content no longer sounds like you, when the objectives are not met after 3 months of serious work, or when communication with the provider becomes a problem in itself. But avoid switching too fast: editorial continuity takes time to build, and outsourced community management requires a phase of mutual learning.
