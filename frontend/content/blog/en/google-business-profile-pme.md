## In brief

Your Google Business Profile listing is your number one storefront on Google. It appears before your website in local results. It is free, it generates calls, directions and visits - provided it is complete and active.

This guide covers everything: creating your listing, verifying it, optimizing it across 8 specific points, publishing posts without spending time on it, and avoiding the mistakes that drag down your local ranking.

---

## Why Google Business Profile is essential in 2026

A few figures that speak for themselves:

- **46%** of Google searches have local intent.
- **97%** of consumers look up a local business online before visiting in person.
- A complete, verified listing generates up to **7x more clicks** than an incomplete one.
- Listings with photos receive **42% more direction requests**.
- On average, a small business is found **1,009 times per month** through its Google Business Profile listing.
- **51%** of local searches trigger an action within 24 hours: a call, a message or a visit.

The Local Pack - the block of 3 local results displayed at the top of Google - captures **44% of clicks** on a results page, compared to 29% for standard organic results.

For a small business, a tradesperson or a self-employed professional, neglecting your Google business listing means handing customers to the competition.

## Creating and verifying your listing: steps in order

### Step 1 - Check whether a listing already exists

Type your business name into Google. If a listing appears with the message "Do you own this business?", click on it to claim it. Do not create a second duplicate listing.

### Step 2 - Create the listing

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com). Sign in with a Google account, then follow the setup wizard:

1. Enter the **exact name** of your business (without adding keywords).
2. Choose your **primary category**.
3. Enter your **address** if customers come to you, or your **service area** if you travel to them.
4. Add your **phone number** and your website URL.

### Step 3 - Verify the listing

Google must confirm that you are the owner. Available verification methods depending on your situation:

- **Postcard** with a code (most common method, 5 to 14 days).
- **SMS or phone call** (available for certain accounts).
- **Email** or **Google Search Console** (if your website is already verified there).
- **Live video** (for new businesses in certain areas).

Until the listing is verified, it will not appear in results. This is the absolute priority.

## The 8 priority optimization points

Once the listing is verified, here is what genuinely makes a difference for local SEO.

| # | Point | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Primary category** | Choose the most specific one possible (e.g. "Plumber" rather than "Tradesperson"). Add 2 to 3 relevant secondary categories. |
| 2 | **Description** | 750 characters max. Who you are, what you do, for whom, and where. Include your keywords naturally, without keyword stuffing. |
| 3 | **Photos** | At least 10 authentic photos: exterior, team, work completed, products. Refresh them regularly. |
| 4 | **Hours** | Keep them up to date at all times, including public holidays and exceptional closures. |
| 5 | **Attributes** | Accessibility, payment methods, services offered, languages spoken. Check everything that applies. |
| 6 | **Questions and Answers (Q&A)** | Post the most frequently asked questions yourself and answer them. Monitor questions left by third parties. |
| 7 | **Customer reviews** | Respond to 100% of reviews, positive and negative, promptly. Request reviews after every job. |
| 8 | **Regular posts** | Publish at least 1 post per week (news, offer, tip, event). Posts expire after 7 days. |

One often overlooked point: **NAP consistency** (Name, Address, Phone). Your contact details must be identical on your Google listing, your website, your social media profiles and your directory listings. The slightest discrepancy muddles your local SEO signals.

## Publishing GBP posts regularly without spending time on it

One post per week means 52 posts per year. For an already overstretched business owner, that can seem like a lot. In practice, it is not that heavy a lift if you structure your approach.

**Three types of posts that work without creative effort:**

- **News**: a recent project completed, a new service, a new hire.
- **Offer**: a seasonal promotion, an introductory rate, a bundle.
- **Tip**: a piece of advice related to your trade, a question your customers frequently ask.

**The quick method:**

1. Put together a [social media editorial calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar) once a month - 30 minutes is enough.
2. Write 4 posts in one sitting and schedule them in advance.
3. Repurpose your existing content: a LinkedIn post, an Instagram photo or a customer email can become a GBP post in 5 minutes.

If you are already managing your [social media as a business owner](/en/blog/social-media-for-business-owners), GBP posts fit naturally into the same workflow. The question of [how often to post on LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook](/en/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media) applies in the same way: consistency matters more than volume.

## Common mistakes that hurt local visibility

These mistakes are widespread. They cost real visibility.

- **Adding keywords to your business name.** This violates Google's guidelines and can result in your listing being suspended.
- **Leaving outdated hours.** A customer who makes a wasted trip will not come back - and will leave a negative review.
- **Ignoring negative reviews.** Not responding makes the situation worse. A calm, professional reply reduces the impact.
- **Never publishing posts.** A listing with no recent activity loses relevance in the eyes of the local algorithm.
- **Forgetting to complete attributes.** Google uses this data to filter results (e.g. "open on Sundays", "wheelchair accessible").
- **Having inconsistent NAP details.** A different address between your website and your Google listing creates confusion that the algorithm penalizes.
- **Neglecting the Q&A section.** Anyone can ask a question there - and answer it. Take control of this section.

If you have considered [outsourcing your social media](/en/blog/delegate-social-media-management) to an agency or intern, note that managing your GBP listing deserves the same attention: a poorly managed listing can do more harm than an inactive one.

## FAQ

**Is Google Business Profile really free?**

Yes, entirely. Creating, verifying and optimizing a Google Business Profile listing costs nothing. Google also offers paid advertising options (local Google Ads), but these are optional.

**What is the difference between Google Business Profile and Google My Business?**

None. Google renamed Google My Business to Google Business Profile in 2022. It is the same tool, with a slightly updated interface. Both terms refer to the same Google business listing.

**How long does it take to optimize your listing?**

Allow 2 to 3 hours for a complete setup (creation, verification, filling in all fields, adding photos). After that, 15 to 30 minutes per week is enough to keep the listing active with posts and review responses.

**Can I have multiple listings if I have multiple locations?**

Yes. Each physical address can have its own Google Business Profile listing. Google even offers a bulk management tool for businesses with more than 10 locations.
