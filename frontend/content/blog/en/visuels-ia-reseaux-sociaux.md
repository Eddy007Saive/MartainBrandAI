## In brief

Artificial intelligence now makes it possible to produce professional visuals without a designer and without Photoshop. But generating a beautiful image is not enough: without visual consistency, your profile looks like a sample catalogue. This article covers available styles, formats by channel, brand guidelines, and the real limitations of AI. It does not cover photo retouching or video creation.

---

## Why visual consistency matters more than individual quality

A beautiful image on its own does not build a brand. What builds a brand is repetition.

When a visitor lands on your Instagram or LinkedIn profile, they see a grid, not a single post. If every image has a different style, a different palette, a different feel, they remember nothing. They scroll on.

The numbers confirm this reality:

- Posts with visual content generate up to **10 times more engagement** than purely text posts (Swello, 2025).
- Visual content is **4 times more memorable** than text.
- On Facebook, a post with an image generates **twice as much engagement** as a post without one.

But engagement also depends on recognition. A brand whose visuals are consistent is identified before the user even reads the account name. This is the series effect: each image reinforces the ones that came before it.

To [create content with AI](/en/blog/ai-content-creation-small-business) effectively, visual consistency is not an aesthetic detail. It is the condition that allows your publishing effort to accumulate rather than scatter.

## AI image styles: which one to choose for your sector

AI image generation offers several distinct aesthetics. Choosing the right style is already 80% of the consistency work.

| Style | Description | Works well for | Feels wrong for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Photorealistic** | Looks like a real photo, natural light, fine details | Trades, retail, healthcare, restaurants | SaaS, abstract services |
| **Cinematic** | Dramatic atmosphere, crafted lighting, depth of field | Storytelling, events, premium brands | Entry-level coaching, highly accessible sectors |
| **3D animated** | Modern, clean, stylised volumes | SaaS, online training, digital services | Craft trades, healthcare professions |
| **Illustration** | Warm, hand-drawn feel, approachable | Education, coaching, wellness, nonprofits | Sectors seeking institutional credibility |
| **Neon** | Electric colours, strong visual impact | Events, nightlife, creative sectors, music | Liberal professions, traditional B2B |
| **Pop art** | Bold colours, off-beat, visual humour | Young brands, humour, food and beverage | Sectors that require sobriety and trust |

The photorealistic style is the most versatile. It reassures, it contextualises, it does not surprise. The other styles are positioning choices: they attract certain audiences and push others away. That is intentional.

## Brand guidelines: why to apply them to every image

Generating an AI image without brand constraints means producing generic visual content. The result looks clean, but it looks like content from your competitors who are using the same tools with the same prompts.

A visual brand identity rests on three elements:

- **The colour palette**: 2 to 3 primary colours that appear on every visual. The eye associates them with your brand without conscious effort.
- **Typography**: the font used in your text visuals. It must be the same everywhere.
- **Visual tone**: the overall atmosphere (light/dark, minimal/busy, human/abstract). This is what gives the impression that all your images come from the same place.

Compare two profiles: one publishes AI images without constraints, a different style every week, colours that keep changing. The other consistently applies its palette and style. After 10 posts, the second is recognisable. The first looks like a test page.

Your editorial line defines what you say. Your visual guidelines define how you show it. Both must be aligned.

## Visual formats to produce by channel

Visual content for small business social media is not one-size-fits-all. Every platform has its own format constraints and its own expectations.

| Channel | Recommended format | Ratio | Visual type that performs | Suggested frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 1080 x 1350 px | 4:5 | Visuals with a stat or quote, carousels | 3 to 5 posts/week |
| **Instagram** | 1080 x 1350 px | 4:5 | Photorealistic, illustration, [Instagram Reels](/en/blog/instagram-reels-small-business) | 4 to 7 posts/week |
| **Facebook** | 1200 x 627 px or 1080 x 1080 px | 1.91:1 or 1:1 | Image with short text, event visual | 3 to 5 posts/week |
| **TikTok** | 1080 x 1920 px | 9:16 | Vertical video, polished short-video thumbnail | 5 to 7 posts/week |
| **Google Business Profile** | 720 x 720 px minimum | 1:1 | Photorealistic product, team, or location photo | 1 to 2 posts/week |

The 4:5 ratio is now the standard for LinkedIn and Instagram feeds: it takes up more screen space than a square, so it captures more attention. The 9:16 ratio is reserved for vertical formats (Stories, Reels, TikTok).

## What AI still does not do well

Let's be direct. AI generates usable images, but it has real limitations you need to know before publishing.

**Text inside images is often unreadable.** Models treat letters as visual shapes, not as language. The result: distorted, reversed, or garbled characters. If your visual needs to include a slogan or a figure, add it in post-production with a layout tool.

**Hands and faces are sometimes distorted.** Extra fingers, incorrect proportions, frozen expressions. On an image of a professional in action, the result can give away the AI generation. Always check before publishing.

**Consistency across multiple images in the same series is fragile.** If you generate 10 images that are supposed to show the same person or the same location, they will not look alike. AI does not remember a character from one generation to the next.

**Quality depends on the prompt.** A vague prompt gives a vague result. The more precise you are about style, atmosphere, colours, and subject, the more usable the result. This is a skill that takes practice.

These limitations do not disqualify the tool. They require human review before every publication.

## How to integrate AI visuals into your publishing workflow

The goal is not to generate one image per post. It is to define a system that runs with minimal effort.

**Step 1: define your style once.**
Choose your image style (photorealistic, illustration, 3D animated...) and document your colour palette. These two decisions apply to all your future generations. You do not revisit them with every post.

**Step 2: generate in batches.**
Rather than generating an image at the moment of publishing, block 30 minutes per week to produce 5 to 10 visuals at once. You then have a ready stock, and you avoid last-minute pressure.

**Step 3: validate before publishing.**
Check text, hands, faces, and consistency with your brand guidelines. A visual that feels off does more harm than good. Human validation is not optional.

## FAQ

**Can AI completely replace a designer for social media?**
For routine production of post visuals, yes, in most cases. For a complete visual identity (logo, brand guidelines, print materials), no. AI is effective for volume and regularity. A designer remains relevant for the founding decisions of a brand.

**Do you need a paid subscription to create quality AI images?**
Free tools exist and allow you to get started. But paid versions offer higher resolutions, more generations per month, and often better customisation options. For regular professional use, a paid subscription is generally justified.

**How do you stop your AI visuals from looking like your competitors'?**
By applying your brand guidelines to every generation: colour palette, fixed style, consistent visual tone. Two businesses using the same tool with different guidelines produce images that are immediately distinct. It is the guidelines that differentiate, not the tool.
